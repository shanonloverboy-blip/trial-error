01 · Meet your robot

One car, many missions

A sturdy 4WD chassis with a brain, eyes and a face.

The robot is controlled by the Keyestudio V4.0 board, an Arduino Uno-compatible board built on the ATmega328P microcontroller. On top of it sits a motor driver expansion board using the DRV8833 chip, which powers the four motors and gives every sensor a plug-in port. The kit comes with 17 learning projects that grow from simple to advanced.

What it can do

  • Follow a black line with the 3-channel line-tracking sensor
  • Measure distance and avoid obstacles with the ultrasonic sensor
  • Follow your hand as you move it in front of the car
  • Stay inside a boundary drawn on the floor
  • Show faces and icons on the 8×16 LED board
  • Drive by IR remote or by phone app (iOS and Android)

03 · Before you start

Set up for success

Ten minutes of preparation saves an hour of searching for screws.

Your workspace

  • A clear, flat table with good light
  • A small tray or bowl for each screw size
  • The screwdriver from the kit
  • About 60–90 minutes for the full build
  • A computer with a USB port, for Part 4

Know your screws

  • M3 vs M2: M2 is the thinner screw. It is only used in a few places.
  • The second number is the length. An M3×6 is short, an M3×30 is long.
  • Pillars are the gold hex spacers that hold boards apart.
  • Lay one of each next to the kit list to compare.

How every part works

Installation

Tip

Tighten screws until they are snug, then stop. Over-tightening can crack the acrylic board or strip the plastic.

Safety

Keep batteries out of the holder until the build is finished. Check the + and − markings every time you insert them. Ask an adult to help with 18650 lithium batteries.

Teamwork

Building in pairs works well: one person reads the step and finds the parts, the other assembles. Swap roles after each part.

Rotate the servo to 90°

The servo turns the ultrasonic sensor left and right. Setting it to its middle position (90°) first means the sensor will face straight ahead once it is mounted. Connect the servo to the board, plug the board into your computer, and upload the servo code.

Option A · Arduino

Download the code: Arduino.7z. Open the servo sketch in the Arduino IDE and upload it.

Option B · KidsBlock

Download the code: Kidsblock.7z. Open the servo project in KidsBlock and upload it.

Important

Choose one option and follow the matching course. If your computer doesn't recognise the board, install the USB driver first (Keyestudio guide, chapter 3).

06 · Final check

Ready for launch?

Go through this list before you put batteries in.

Build

  • All four wheels turn freely and don't rub the chassis
  • Every board sits flat on its pillars
  • No screws are loose and none are left over
  • The ultrasonic sensor faces straight ahead

Wiring

  • M1 → B1, M2 → B, M3 → A, M4 → A1
  • Line-tracking, ultrasonic, LED board and servo are plugged in
  • Battery lead is in the BAT port
  • Wires are tied back clear of the wheels

First power-on

  1. Insert the batteries, checking + and − against the holder.
  2. Remove the Bluetooth module, connect the USB cable and upload your first project.
  3. Unplug USB, refit the Bluetooth module and switch on.
  4. Lift the car off the table the first time the motors run, so it can't drive off the edge.

If something isn't working

What you seeWhat to check
Code won't uploadTake out the Bluetooth module. Check the driver is installed and the right port is selected.
A wheel spins the wrong way or not at allCheck that motor's plug is in the port listed in Part 8.
Sensor looks sidewaysRe-run the 90° servo code from Part 4 and refit the cradle head.
Nothing turns onCheck battery direction, battery charge and the BAT connection.
